## 0. How to read this document

### CLAIMED vs VERIFIED

Every performance number in this document is **CLAIMED (unverified)** unless explicitly stated otherwise.
We have not reproduced a single backtest here. Nothing in this file is evidence that a strategy works.
It is a catalogue of *hypotheses other people assert*, recorded so we can test the underlying ideas
ourselves on our own data with our own cost model.

Three tiers of trust are used:

| Tier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A — Peer-reviewed / working paper with stated method** | Published academic work with a stated sample, method, and significance testing. Still unverified by us, but the methodology is inspectable and the incentive to lie is lower. Note: academic results are still subject to publication bias and rarely model retail spread. |
| **B — Open-source / reproducible artifact** | Public code or public data. Claims are unverified but *auditable* — we could rerun them. |
| **C — Marketing / forum / vendor claim** | No method, no sample, or a product for sale behind the number. Treated as a *pointer to an idea*, never as evidence. |

### Our binding constraints (used in the compatibility columns)

- Instrument: XAUUSDm (Exness), 100 oz contract. **0.01 lot = 1 oz → $1 price move = $1.00 P/L.**
- Account: **$100**. Fixed 0.01 lot. Minimum risk per trade at a 1.5×ATR stop ≈ **$15.84 (~15.8% of account)**.
- Therefore: **~5 consecutive losses is account death.** Sequence risk dominates expectancy.
- Spread: Exness Standard gold spread commonly quoted ~16–25 cents/oz ⇒ **~$0.16–$0.25 per round turn at 0.01 lot** (CLAIMED, broker/comparison-site sourced). This is ~1–1.5% of a $15.84 stop — *tolerable for wide-stop strategies, fatal for scalps*.
- Cost-sensitivity rule of thumb derived from the above: a strategy with a **$2 stop** pays ~10% of risk in spread; a strategy with a **$16 stop** pays ~1.3%. **Wide stops are the only spread-survivable regime at this account size.**
- Consequence: we need **low frequency, high R:R, session-specialised, wide-stop** systems. This filter is applied in every "Compatibility" cell.

## 3. Concepts worth testing on our data

Extracted as **hypotheses**, deliberately stripped of the source's specific parameters. Parameters from external sources are assumed overfit; only the *mechanism* is imported. Each is scored against our four constraints: session-specialised, low frequency, wide R:R, low spread sensitivity.

### H1 — Session-conditional behaviour is the primary structure (foundation, test first)
**Hypothesis:** Gold's intraday statistical properties (realised range, trend persistence, mean-reversion, spread) differ materially and stably by hour/session, and any strategy must be built *inside* one regime rather than across all 24 hours.
**Support:** A1 (Tier A, 15yr 5-min, n-shaped volume peaking 11:00–17:00 GMT), A2, A19, A20; corroborated qualitatively by R25.
**First experiment (not a strategy — a measurement):** on our own tick data, compute per-hour-GMT: realised range, spread (mean **and 95th percentile**), variance ratio / trend persistence, and P(daily high or low is set in this hour). This validates or destroys the widely-repeated "70% of daily extremes in the overlap" claim (R25) which currently has **zero methodology behind it**.
**Constraint fit:** ✅ session ✅ low freq ✅ — this is the prerequisite for everything else.

### H2 — Asian-range compression → London expansion breakout
**Hypothesis:** The low-volatility Asian session forms a compressed range; the London open injects order flow that breaks and extends it. Entry on a break of the Asian range, stop on the opposite side or at k×ATR, target a multiple of the range.
**Support:** R9, R10, R11 (Tier C, saturated but mechanistically coherent), consistent with A1's volume profile and A7's "ORB works where intraday trending exists".
**Constraint fit:** ✅ session-specialised ✅ **~1 trade/day → low frequency** ✅ stop = range width → naturally wide → **spread ≈1% of risk** ✅ R:R naturally ≥2 if target is a range multiple.
**Caveats:** Most crowded archetype in gold. **Must include a range-width filter** — breaking out of an abnormally *wide* Asian range gives an unaffordable stop; breaking out of an abnormally *narrow* one gives whipsaw. Test the *conditional* version: breakout only when the Asian range is in a middle percentile band of its own trailing distribution.
**Priority: HIGHEST.** Best constraint fit of anything found.

### H3 — Overnight/day session return reversal
**Hypothesis:** A large Asian-session directional move is partially reversed during London/NY.
**Support:** **A8 (Tier A — "very strong negative autocorrelation between overnight and intraday returns in gold")**, reinforced by A20 (11.6M observations) and A21 (mechanism: liquidity oversupply from uninformed flow gets faded).
**Constraint fit:** ✅ one signal per day ✅ session-specialised ✅ entry at a stretched price means the stop can sit beyond a session extreme (wide, well-defined) ✅ target = return to session VWAP/open (large).
**Note:** **H2 and H3 are direct opposites** (breakout vs fade). That is a feature: run both on the same data with the same cost model. If neither wins, the session has no edge; if one wins conditionally on range width or on macro-news presence, that conditioning *is* the strategy. This is the cleanest adversarial test available to us.
**Priority: HIGHEST (paired with H2).**

### H4 — Liquidity sweep of a prior session / prior day extreme, then reclaim
**Hypothesis:** Price trades through a well-known reference level (previous day high/low, Asian high/low, round $10/$25/$50 number), triggers clustered stops, then reclaims the level — the reclaim is the entry, the sweep extreme is the stop, the opposing reference level is the target.
**Support:** R1 (Tier B, public code, CLAIMED 71% WR on 4,547 trades — but **no costs, no stop, no PF**); A12/A13 (Tier A, round-number barriers in gold, with A13 as a caution); A21 (Tier A mechanism); stripped-down version of R19/ICT.
**Constraint fit:** ✅ **stop is defined by a real structural extreme, so it is wide** ✅ target is a distant structural level → **R:R often 2–4** ✅ spread-tolerant. ⚠️ frequency: R1's ~230/yr is too high; a session filter is required to cut it.
**Mandatory changes to R1's version:** add a **hard price stop** (R1 uses only an end-of-day time stop — an unbounded loss, which is instant death for us), and model costs.
**Priority: HIGH.**

### H5 — Volatility-normalised noise-band breakout (adaptive threshold, not a fixed range)
**Hypothesis:** Define the day's expected move from the **trailing N-day average intraday range**; a move beyond that envelope signals a genuine imbalance rather than noise.
**Support:** A4 (Zarattini et al., Tier A/B, SPY, CLAIMED Sharpe 1.33 net of costs).
**Why it matters for us:** it is an **adaptive, self-scaling threshold with essentially one parameter (N)**, which is far more overfit-resistant than a fixed pip/dollar band. Directly addresses R12's warning that fixed configurations tuned on 2023–2026 gold fail in 2013/2020/2022, since the band rescales with the volatility regime.
**Constraint fit:** ✅ wide stops by construction ✅ low frequency (only genuine outliers trigger) ✅ session-compatible (compute the envelope per session).
**Priority: HIGH — and it should be the *threshold mechanism* used inside H2 and H4 rather than a separate strategy.**

### H6 — Time-of-day-aware volatility normalisation (methodology, not a strategy)
**Hypothesis:** A single 24-hour ATR misprices risk at every hour. Stops, filters and position logic should use an **hour-of-day-adjusted** volatility estimate.
**Support:** A19 (Andersen & Bollerslev — deterministic intraday periodicity dominates intraday volatility), A1.
**Why it matters for us specifically:** our entire risk model is "1.5×ATR ⇒ $15.84". If ATR is computed across all hours, the 1.5× multiplier is systematically too wide at 03:00 GMT and too tight at 14:00 GMT. **This single correction may change our results more than any strategy choice.**
**Priority: HIGH — implement before further strategy testing.**

### H7 — Session-opening block sets the session's direction
**Hypothesis:** The first N minutes of the session that *first prices new information* carries predictive power for the remainder of that session.
**Support:** A3 (Tier A, JFE — first half-hour predicts last half-hour, stronger on news days and high-vol days), A9 (Tier A 2025 — predictive block *moves* to whichever session first absorbs international news), A5/A6/A7 (ORB across markets).
**Adaptation for 24h spot gold:** there is no single "open". The candidate information-pricing blocks are **London open (~07:00 GMT)** and **COMEX/NY open (~13:30 GMT)**. Test both.
**Constraint fit:** ✅ session ✅ 1–2 signals/day. ⚠️ A3's raw effect size is small relative to a 15.8%-of-account stop — use it as a **direction filter for H2/H4**, not a standalone entry.
**Priority: MEDIUM-HIGH (as a filter).**

### H8 — Structural clock events: 10:30 and 15:00 London (LBMA fix auctions)
**Hypothesis:** Volume and volatility reliably cluster at the LBMA auction times; these are structural, scheduled liquidity events, not anomalies.
**Support:** A16 (Tier A / NBER — significant volume and volatility increases at the start of the afternoon fix).
**Use:** two applications. (a) as **session boundaries** for range/breakout definitions; (b) as an **avoidance window** if we find our stops are disproportionately hit there.
**Explicit warning:** the historically documented *directional* (downward) afternoon-fix anomaly is attributed to conduct that was prosecuted and reformed. **Do not trade the historical direction.** Only the volatility clustering is expected to persist.
**Priority: MEDIUM.**

### H9 — Macro-news as a conditioning variable (mostly for avoidance)
**Hypothesis:** Scheduled macro releases (FOMC, NFP, CPI) systematically alter both the return process and the *cost* process.
**Support:** A10 (Tier A — gold adjustment continues >5 min after FOMC shocks, asymmetric, implying drift); A3 (predictability stronger on news days); R2 implements a ±30 min blackout; R14 and R12 both say news trading is where spreads punish retail hardest.
**Constraint fit for trading it:** ❌ spread and slippage are worst exactly then; event count is tiny.
**Constraint fit for avoiding it:** ✅✅ a news blackout **reduces frequency and removes the worst-cost trades** — both of which we want.
**Priority: implement the blackout (HIGH); trading the drift (LOW).**

### H10 — Daily / multi-week trend as a one-way filter
**Hypothesis:** Only take intraday signals aligned with the higher-timeframe gold trend.
**Support:** A11 (Tier A, time-series momentum, 58 instruments, >25 yrs — though with well-documented post-2010 decay); R14's finding that trend rules "held up longest" among 4,000 gold timing rules; R2 uses an EMA200 filter.
**Constraint fit:** ✅ adds zero trades, only removes them ✅ reduces frequency.
**Caveat:** halves the sample, which hurts statistical power on an already small trade count. Test as an *ablation* on top of a working base strategy — never build it in from the start.
**Priority: MEDIUM.**

### H11 — Range-width / volatility-regime conditioning
**Hypothesis:** Breakout systems only work when the pre-breakout range is *compressed relative to its own recent distribution*; fade systems only work when the move is *extended* relative to it.
**Support:** volatility clustering is one of the most robust facts in finance; A1's returns↔volatility Granger causality; R2's ATR volatility floor; R11's explicit rationale.
**Constraint fit:** ✅ **this is the single best frequency-reduction lever we have** — it converts "one trade every day" into "one trade on the ~30% of days where the setup is real", which is exactly the trade-count profile a $100 account needs.
**Priority: HIGH — apply as a gate on H2/H3/H4.**

### H12 — Day-of-week conditioning
**Hypothesis:** Intraday gold behaviour varies by weekday (e.g. Monday volatility jump, quiet Friday).
**Support:** A14 (Tier A — significant Monday jump in gold implied vol), A15; R26 (Tier C, unsourced: Tuesday most volatile, Friday least).
**Constraint fit:** ✅ free frequency reduction if real.
**Caveat:** calendar anomalies are **the most data-snooped area in the literature** (A17, A18). Only accept a weekday effect if it survives a multiple-testing correction across all 5 days.
**Priority: LOW-MEDIUM — measure, don't assume.**

### H13 — Cost-model realism as a first-class test (methodology)
**Hypothesis:** Most published gold edges are artifacts of zero- or fixed-cost backtesting.
**Support:** R31 (TradingView defaults to zero commission); R1 ("no costs, perfect execution, no slippage"); R21 (~$0.022 net per trade over 47k trades — inside the spread); R12 (spreads vary several-fold across brokers and across the day); R14 (spread/execution named as failure cause #1); R28 (Exness Standard gold ≈16–25 c/oz).
**Required practice:** never report a single-spread result. Report every candidate at **realistic, elevated, and stressed spread**, plus slippage, and report the **spread at which the edge dies**. A strategy whose edge dies at 1.5× the advertised spread is not a strategy.
**Priority: MANDATORY.**

### H14 — Multiple-testing correction (methodology)
**Hypothesis:** Our own search will manufacture false edges at the rate the literature predicts.
**Support:** **A17 (Sullivan/Timmermann/White), A18 (Hansen SPA, Qi & Wu 2,127 rules)**, and R14's report of **>4,000 gold timing rules whose apparent winners vanished after correcting for luck**.
**Required practice:** log every configuration tried (`docs/research/RESEARCH_LEDGER.md` is the right home), and score the best result against a bootstrap/SPA null built from the *full* universe searched — not against zero.
**Priority: MANDATORY.**

### H15 — Out-of-sample regime coverage (methodology)
**Hypothesis:** Gold's 2023–2026 bull run makes long-biased and breakout logic look far better than it is.
**Support:** R12, explicitly and repeatedly; R14's recency/overfitting section.

### Point 5 — Is $20/day (≈19%/day) on a $105 account achievable by any credible sustained strategy?

**Short answer: No. Not as a sustained daily expectation. It is roughly 50–70× larger than the best-documented, largest-sample, survivorship-bias-free day-trading returns that exist in the public literature, and it does not survive even one round of compounding logic.**

The numbers, lined up:

| Source | Population / sample | Sustained daily return |
|---|---|---|
| Barber/Odean Taiwan, **average day trader** (B3) | 15 years, full exchange population, millions of trades | **−0.24%/day** (net loss) |
| Barber/Odean Taiwan, **top 500 traders** (top ≈0.1–0.5% of population) (B3) | Same 15-year population | **+0.28–0.38%/day net** — the best large-sample, non-survivorship-biased figure found in either research pass |
| ESMA/AMF regulatory disclosures (B4) | ~15,000+ EU retail CFD/FX accounts | 74–89% unprofitable over the period; profitable minority's *rate* not disclosed, but 5–7% remain profitable after 5 years |
| Prop-firm industry aggregate figures (B5, Tier C, directional only) | Aggregator-stated | "conservative" ≈0.05–0.1%/day, "moderate/funded" ≈0.1–0.2%/day; ~7% of accounts ever reach payout |
| **Our own live-validated 4-leg backtest** (context given, not from this research pass) | Tier-2 M1 backtest, PF 1.512 | **≈6.6%/day** ($7 on $105.74) |
| **Target** | — | **≈19%/day** ($20 on $105) |

Even our own already-validated backtest result (~6.6%/day) sits roughly **17–24× above** the best rigorously-measured sustained day-trading return in the literature (the Taiwan top-500's 0.28–0.38%/day), and the **$20/day target is ~3× our own result again**, i.e. ~50-70x the best documented external benchmark. Three independent reasons this should be treated as a hard reality check rather than a "just optimize more" problem:

1. **Compounding arithmetic makes 19%/day absurd as a stated target even before any strategy question.** 19%/day sustained for just 20 trading days (one month) is >97% percent growth compounded, and for a full year it is a number with no correspondence to any real financial return ever recorded by a retail account over a multi-year period. A target expressed as "$20/day" implicitly assumes the number stays flat in dollar terms as the account grows — which is a *different and far more defensible* claim (roughly 19% of $105, but a much smaller percentage of a $1,000 or $10,000 account) than "19%/day sustained." **This is worth being explicit about internally: is the real goal "$20/day forever regardless of account size" (a flat-dollar income target, decreasingly aggressive in % terms as the account grows) or "19%/day as a rate" (impossible)?** These are completely different problems and the file's framing should not conflate them.
2. **Point 1's own math (B10) suggests we may already be under-pricing survival risk at our current PF, before even discussing scaling up toward $20/day.** A 5-in-a-row loss streak at ~15% risk per trade is a near-certainty multiple times a year at ~230 trades/yr base rates, and removes ~44-55% of the account each time it happens. Pushing frequency or size up to chase a 3x-higher $ target directly increases the odds and severity of exactly this failure mode.
3. **The best-documented elite day traders' edge (Taiwan top 500, B3) comes from covering large transaction costs on a *skill* basis across a huge trade count (survival to 3 years is only 15% of the population) — not from any single strategy or session pattern.** No credible source found in either research pass — academic, regulatory, or vendor — reports a *sustained*, *verified*, *cost-inclusive* daily return anywhere near 19%/day for any retail-sized account. Every source claiming numbers in that range (pass 1's R17 "+541% in 5 months," R18 "287.92% verified gains") was flagged as a red-flag exemplar (survivorship, undisclosed risk, likely martingale/leverage) rather than a credible result.

**Recommendation for the parent project:** treat $20/day as either (a) an aspirational flat-dollar income target to be revisited once the account has grown well past $100–500 (at which point 19%/day becomes a much smaller, though still aggressive, percentage), or (b) explicitly re-derive it as a percentage-of-current-equity target and compare that percentage, not the dollar figure, against the ~0.3–1%/day ceiling this research found for credible sustained edges. As a **daily rate on a $105 account starting today, $20/day is not supported as achievable by any credible source found in this research, and the gap is roughly 50-70x, not the "~3x" framing in the task brief** — the 3x gap was between our backtested $7/day and the $20 target; the 50-70x gap is between the best external evidence for what is *sustainably achievable at all* and the $20 target.
